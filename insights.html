What the data says

medicaid exclusion statistics · figures as of October 31, 2026 · reproducible from workbench §15–§17

Every number on this page was computed from the RosterProof unified exclusion dataset — the federal OIG LEIE, the full SAM.gov exclusions extract, two FDA lists and 42 state Medicaid exclusion lists plus the District of Columbia (47 sources), resolved to NPIs against the CMS NPPES registry. This edition refreshes the July 2026 findings against data as of October 31, 2026; where a July figure still tells the story, it is shown as then → now. Each figure is reproducible from the published dataset and carries its own caveat.

How this data is collected, matched and quality-checked: read the Methodology (PDF, 2 pages).

Two ways to measure what a federal-only check misses

Measure A: 54.1% — share of active state exclusion records with no matching record on the federal OIG LEIE. All active state records, matched by NPI where both sides carry one, otherwise by normalized name. July: 54.5% → now 54.1%.

Measure B: 62.6% — share of active state records with an NPPES-verified NPI whose NPI appears on neither the LEIE nor SAM.gov. 14,735 of 23,538 verified-NPI active state records.

Two different questions: A covers every state record including the name-only ones; B counts only records whose identity the registry confirms, and checks both federal lists. Per-state figures for both: the state index; how A is measured: the federal gap, measured.

How we count it — five steps, nothing clever:

  1. Active only. The excluding state has published no end date (or a future one) and still carries the record on its list.
  2. Proven identity only. The record carries an NPI confirmed against the CMS NPPES registry. Name-only records are not counted.
  3. Count excluding states. Federal lists don't count as a state. One state is enough; the "two or more" figure is the subset.
  4. Look up where they practice now — the practice-location state on their NPPES record.
  5. Keep the mismatches. Practice state not among the excluding states.

Caveats, so the number holds up: NPPES practice state is self-reported and can lag a move. "Has not excluded" means absent from that state's published list — several states publish no usable list, so there is nothing for the provider to appear on. And this counts only providers pinned to a verified NPI; most exclusion records are name-only and are not counted here, so the true figure is larger.

Honest boundary: same-NPI tracking is what exclusion-list data can prove — a provider who abandons their identity behind a new organizational NPI is invisible to this method. Detecting that requires ownership linkage, which we name as future work rather than claim.

…and the companies dominate the categories that bill at scale: 100% of excluded hospice agencies and 94% of excluded labs are organizations, while license-driven exclusions are 99.5% individual — licenses attach to people.

When a company goes multi-state, it lands in many states at once. Organizations are 5.2% of two-jurisdiction excludees but 16.2% of four-plus (July: 4.8% → 20.4%).

Caveat: type shares among NPI-verified matches; category percentages use NPPES enumeration type (Type 1 person / Type 2 organization).

How matches are made. Of 58,771 NPI-resolved records, 98.0% are matched deterministically (a source-published NPI the registry confirms, a unique name-and-state or registered-alias match, or a license number); the remaining 2.0% (1,150) are AI-adjudicated among ambiguous candidates, and every one of those is flagged for human review. In all, 6,525 records (1.9%) carry the needs-human-review flag.

Medicare Opt-Out — a status, not an exclusion

57,653 Medicare opt-out affidavits (53,437 active) are published as a separate view. An opt-out means the provider has chosen to contract privately with Medicare patients; it is not an exclusion or sanction, is never counted in any figure above, and never appears as a hit. It is there so a roster check can tell the two apart.
