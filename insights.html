Excluded somewhere, practicing where nobody can see it

The actionable number. Take every provider we have proven is excluded right now (matched to a verified NPI), check where the national NPI registry says they currently practice, and keep the ones practicing in a state that has not excluded them. A clinic or health plan in that state, screening against its own state list, sees a clean provider.

A real one, initials only. Dr. K.B. is a family-medicine physician. Eight states carry an active exclusion on her — California, Colorado, Kansas, Michigan, Missouri, New Jersey, New York and Pennsylvania — the first in March 2023, the latest in September 2025. The national NPI registry says she practices in Tennessee. Tennessee has not excluded her, and she is not on the federal list either. A Tennessee Medicaid plan that checks its own state list and the federal LEIE sees nothing. Under ACA §6501 every one of those eight exclusions is grounds for termination in Tennessee too.

How we count it — five steps, nothing clever:

  1. Active only. The excluding state has published no end date (or a future one) and still carries the record on its list. Reinstated or expired exclusions are out.
  2. Proven identity only. The record carries an NPI confirmed against the CMS NPPES registry. Name-only records are not counted — a name alone could be two people.
  3. Count excluding states. Federal lists don't count as a state. One state is enough; the "two or more" figure above is the subset.
  4. Look up where they practice now — the practice-location state on their NPPES record.
  5. Keep the mismatches. Practice state not among the excluding states.

Caveats, so the number holds up: NPPES practice state is self-reported by the provider and can lag a move. "Has not excluded" means absent from that state's published list — several states (Florida among them) publish no usable list, so there is nothing for the provider to appear on. And this counts only providers we can pin to a verified NPI; roughly three quarters of all exclusion records are name-only and are not counted here, so the true figure is larger.

And what changes when they move? About 1 in 30 multi-state excludees appears under materially different surnames in different states — sometimes a legal name change, sometimes the states themselves misspelling the same person three different ways. Either way, name-based screening loses. Identity has to follow the NPI.

Honest boundary: same-NPI tracking is what exclusion-list data can prove — a provider who abandons their identity behind a new organizational NPI or corporate shell is invisible to this method. Detecting that requires ownership linkage, which we name as future work rather than claim.

People and companies

93%
of healthcare exclusions are people
7%
are companies

…and the companies dominate the categories that bill at scale: 100% of excluded hospice agencies and 94% of excluded labs are organizations, while license-driven exclusions are 99.5% individual — licenses attach to people.

When a company goes multi-state, it lands everywhere at once. Organizations are 4.8% of two-state excludees but 20.4% of four-plus-state ones — mass multi-state enrollments mean an excluded company shows up in many states nearly simultaneously.

Caveat: type shares among NPI-verified matches; category percentages use NPPES enumeration type (Type 1 person / Type 2 organization).

Zero person/company conflicts across all 25,206 registry-verified matches — the entity-type invariant held end-to-end. When we say a match is verified, the registry agrees about what kind of thing it is, every single time.
